# Make product decisions when they become relevant

> Working proposal for review, recorded from the founder's direction on 2026-11-03. Publication here does not by itself establish adopted policy or implemented product behavior.

Make major product and design choices as late as practical, when the choice is relevant and the people deciding have real context. Keep options open while learning is useful and affordable. Commit when the next meaningful step needs a choice, or when waiting would close an option, create avoidable harm, or cost more than the expected learning.

This extends the founder's Lean Startup approach: build a small useful slice, learn from use, and revise the next choice from evidence. Planning should identify what to learn and when a decision becomes necessary, rather than turn hypothetical answers into permanent commitments. Complete certainty is neither available nor required.

## In practice

- **Start from the actual case.** Identify the person's current goal, workflow, constraints and affected participants. Use relevant observations, prototypes, tests or case studies. State important unknowns and distinguish observed evidence from assumptions; do not invent a case study to fill a gap.
- **Separate direction from commitment.** A purpose or desired outcome can guide work now while an implementation choice stays open. Choose the smallest reversible default or experiment that makes progress. State its limits and how to undo or replace it; an expensive migration or a promise to others is not reversible just because a setting can change.
- **Name the trigger for an important deferred choice.** Say what real event, evidence or dependency makes it necessary, who will bring it back, and the latest safe decision point when one exists. Do not repeatedly ask people to settle choices that do not yet affect their work. Do not let a temporary default quietly become permanent through growing dependencies.
- **Decide with the context at that point.** Present the relevant evidence, realistic options, recommendation and consequences to the person with authority. Preserve the user's ability to choose or revise. Agents may prepare evidence and perform already-authorized work; relevance, learned preferences, silence and temporary defaults do not grant new authority.
- **Revisit when reality changes.** If use contradicts an assumption, circumstances change or the default becomes costly to reverse, reassess it. Keep the previous reasoning and what changed in the existing issue or decision record. A prior plan is evidence to consider, not a reason to continue a suboptimal choice.

## Decide early enough to protect people and options

Waiting is useful only while it preserves a better choice. Decide before an irreversible action, an external commitment or a dependent step makes alternatives impractical. Account for lead times: a deadline to deliver may require a decision well before delivery.

Safety, security, privacy, consent, accessibility obligations and applicable legal requirements constrain experiments from the start. An urgent problem may need an immediate bounded response using the best available evidence, with its uncertainty and review point recorded. Do not postpone necessary protection while waiting for a fuller case study. This principle changes decision timing, not approval requirements or who may act.

## Keep the record proportionate

Use the existing product issue, PR or decision record. A few lines are enough for a consequential open choice: **what remains open; current evidence and uncertainty; temporary default and reversal; trigger or latest safe point; owner and decision authority.** On deciding, add the choice and what would cause a revisit. Routine, low-cost choices need no separate record, new board or approval ceremony.

Illustrative applications, not adopted component decisions:

- **User setup:** let someone try a useful workflow before asking for preferences that matter only to a later feature. Ask when that feature is used, unless setup is necessary for safe operation. Keep defaults visible and changeable.
- **Shared interfaces:** use a thin, replaceable adapter for a first slice. Revisit a general contract when a second real consumer exposes compatibility needs, before publishing an interface others will depend on. Set identity, authorization and data-handling constraints before the slice runs.
- **Exofab pilot:** choose supported company forms and locations from the actual pilot participants' needs and qualified advice when those choices become necessary, before any commitment or filing. Keep a hypothetical universal rollout open. Existing component proposals and approval requirements remain separate.
